# Part-of-Speech Analysis

Use this procedure only when a word's grammatical role, approved part of speech, or approved meaning is uncertain. Do not perform a full part-of-speech analysis for routine Agent/Strict rewrites.

## Purpose

ASD-STE100 controls both meaning and part of speech. A word that is approved as a noun is not automatically approved as a verb. Part-of-speech analysis prevents an apparently simple rewrite from using an approved spelling in an unapproved grammatical role.

## Procedure

1. Copy the complete source sentence. Keep its context.
2. Identify the sentence subject, finite verb, objects, complements, modifiers, conjunctions, and prepositions.
3. Mark the role of the word under review in this sentence.
4. Identify the intended meaning of that word. Do not select a dictionary entry by spelling alone.
5. Check the applicable official dictionary entry for both the approved meaning and part of speech.
6. Check whether the word is an approved technical name or technical verb in the project terminology.
7. If the use is not approved, select an approved construction that preserves the original meaning.
8. Reanalyze the complete revised sentence. A replacement can change the role of nearby words.
9. Record unresolved cases in the compliance report.

## Analysis Record

Use this compact record when the user requests an audit:

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Source sentence | Complete sentence under review |
| Word | Exact word form |
| Contextual meaning | Meaning in this sentence |
| Contextual part of speech | Noun, verb, adjective, adverb, or other role |
| Dictionary status | Approved, not approved, or not verified |
| Technical-term status | Approved project term, not a project term, or not verified |
| Decision | Keep, replace, restructure, or escalate |
| Reason | Meaning and rule basis |

## Common Checks

### Noun or Verb

In `Apply oil to the valve`, `oil` is a noun. In `Oil the valve`, `oil` is a verb. Do not assume that both uses are approved. Check the official dictionary and project terminology.

### Participle or Adjective

A word that ends in `-ed` can be part of a verb phrase or can describe a state. Analyze its function in the complete sentence before applying a verb-form rule.

### Gerund, Participle, or Technical Name

A word that ends in `-ing` can function as a noun, modifier, or verb form. Determine its grammatical role and then check whether the applicable standard or project dictionary permits that use.

### Ambiguous Modifier Attachment

In a long noun phrase, a modifier can attach to more than one noun. Expand the phrase only after you identify the intended relationship. Do not guess which component the modifier describes.

### Coordinated Words

Confirm that coordinated words have parallel roles. A list that mixes an action, an object, and a condition is difficult to parse and can conceal missing sentence parts.

## Replacement Rules

- Preserve meaning before improving grammar.
- Do not select a replacement only because it is shorter.
- Do not replace a precise technical name with a general word.
- Reuse the selected term for the same concept throughout the document.
- If no verified replacement preserves the meaning, keep the source term and mark it for terminology review.
